Association for Education Finance and Policy · Annual Meeting 2026

Project Overview

This poster presents findings from my independent research project: Beyond Classroom Engagement: How Student-Initiated Faculty Interactions Shape Academic Success. Using the Education Longitudinal Study of 2002 (ELS:02), I examined whether increased frequency of student-initiated out-of-class faculty contact predicts higher post-secondary GPA, controlling for prior academic achievement and socioeconomic status.

My Contributions

  • Conceptualized the research question, conducted the full literature review, and developed the analytic framework independently under the advising of Dr. Walter Ecton.
  • Performed OLS regressions with a complete-case analytic strategy, examining first-year GPA, second-year GPA, first-institution GPA, last-institution GPA, STEM GPA, and non-STEM GPA as outcomes.
  • Designed the poster layout, data visualizations (violin plots, regression tables), and narrative structure.
  • Presented and defended findings at the 2026 AEFP annual meeting.

Key Findings

  • First Contact Premium: The largest GPA gain occurs when students move from “Never” to “Sometimes” contacting faculty—a consistent +2 to 3 percentile point boost, with diminishing returns beyond that threshold.
  • Front-Loaded Impact: Effects are strongest during the first year and at the first institution attended.
  • Distribution-Neutral but Gap-Preserving: The academic return on faculty contact is identical regardless of SES or race, but because higher-SES students initiate contact more frequently, baseline interactions raise average achievement without closing structural equity gaps.

Creative Approach

Designed the poster around a three-column narrative arc: Background & Objectives → Key Findings → Discussion. Used violin plots to make distributional shifts immediately visible, and a limited maize-and-navy color palette to direct attention. The equity argument was placed prominently in the Discussion column so viewers encountered it as the culminating insight rather than a footnote.
